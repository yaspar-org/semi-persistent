# acgen: deviation ledger

Source: generated by `egglog/egglog-bridge/examples/make-ac-bench.py` at
7b1adf2, the repository's only benchmark aimed squarely at AC closure: N
integer literals, one left-nested and one right-nested Add chain over them in
opposite orders, reassociation and commutation rules, `(run 10000)` to
fixpoint, and the check that the two chains meet.

Files: `acgen.egglog.egg` (the generated program), `acgen.rules.egg` (ours,
same two rules), `acgen.native.egg` (ours, `Add` variadic `:assoc-comm`, the
two rules deleted), this ledger.

## Deviations

1. **Instance size N=11.** The generator's constant is N=13; the shipped
   instance regenerates with `N=11` (edit the script's first assignment). At
   N=13 the closure costs 34.8 s on egglog and 253 s / 6 452 047 nodes on our
   rules encoding in single runs, which prices a 12-run campaign out; at N=11
   both engines finish in seconds. The N=13 single-run numbers stay recorded
   here as the scaling observation.
2. **Dialect port: `set` becomes `union`.** The generator emits
   `(set (Add b a) e)` actions, which the pinned egglog rejects on a
   constructor ("Cannot set constructor Add. Use `union` instead"). Both the
   egglog copy and our translation use `union`. The generator targets the
   egglog-bridge dialect; the port is what makes the program run on the
   pinned tests dialect at all.
3. `(print-size)` appended on our side for the harness.

## Validation and single-run measurements (N=11)

The check passes on both engines, under both of our strategies, in both
encodings.

| configuration | wall (1 run) | nodes |
|---|---|---|
| egglog | 1.2 s | |
| ours, rules, naive | 10.2 s | 561 336 |
| ours, rules, semi-naive | 24.6 s | 561 336 |
| ours, native, naive | 0.03 s | 41 |

In this single historical run, the rules encoding took 8.5x egglog's wall
time; the native encoding flattens both chains to one multiset at construction,
so the check holds before any rule fires: 41 nodes, and the run is
startup-dominated. In the later, loaded-host `final-r6` snapshot, medians were
812.1 ms for egglog, 6793.3/16439.1 ms for our rules strategies, and 8.1/7.3 ms
for our native strategies. Neither measurement used Criterion or bootstrap
confidence intervals, and neither is evidence about the current implementation.

Both programs end with `(print-stats :file "...")`. Egglog's timed
`no-messages` runs suppress the cardinality output, so the r6 runner reads
iterations from JSON and performs one untimed normal-mode cardinality probe.
