## Lean formalization status: AC-congruence closure

Project `lean/` next to the overleaf clone, Lean 4.35.0 with Mathlib v4.35.0.
`lake build` completes with no `sorry` under `SemperProofs/CC/`. The axiom
audit in `SemperProofs/CC/Check.lean` reports only `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` for every final theorem. The
development formalizes the proof sketch in the section "AC-congruence
closure: soundness and completeness, proof sketch" above; the sketch's
lemma numbers L1 to L16 appear in the docstrings.

### Files

- `SemperProofs/CC/Defs.lean`: the model. `Monomial n` is `Multiset (Fin n)`.
  `degLex` is the engine's order: size first, then the count at the largest
  class where the two differ. `Laws` packages the operator laws as a
  relation `lawRel`, a normalization `clamp` with `clamp M ≤ M`, and a
  `cancellative` flag. `E L S` is the equational theory generated by the
  asserted equalities `S`, closed under congruence with multiset union,
  the law instances and cancellation. `Rw L R` is one clamp-normalized
  rewrite step. `State n` has `find`, `nodes`, `retired` and `rhs`; its
  `rules` are the active nodes whose monomial is `degLex`-greater than the
  class's right-hand side (the read-time orientation guard). `Infer L` has
  the inference kinds `mint`, `joinMerge`, `cancelMerge`, `retire`,
  `setRhs`. Invariants `I0` (the right-hand side is a node of its class),
  `I2` (classes are `=_E`-equal), `I3`, `I4 pool` (fixed summand pool),
  `I5` (the right-hand side is the least active monomial). `Converged`
  and `RoundFixed`.
- `SemperProofs/CC/Lemmas.lean`: L1 to L10 and L12 to L15.
- `SemperProofs/CC/Proofs/CriticalPairModulo.lean`: L11, corrected.
- `SemperProofs/CC/Proofs/RunStabilizes.lean`: L16.
- `SemperProofs/CC/Slot.lean`: the slot model and the sufficiency of the
  refresh fix.
- `SemperProofs/CC/Main.lean`: the final theorems under their final names.
- `SemperProofs/CC/Check.lean`: the axiom audit.

### Theorems

- `degLex_irrefl`, `degLex_trans`, `degLex_trichotomous`,
  `degLex_isStrictTotalOrder` (L1): `degLex` is a strict total order.
- `degLex_wf` (L2): `degLex` is well-founded; size first, and the count
  vectors of one size over `Fin n` are a well-founded colex order
  (`Pi.Lex.wellFounded`).
- `degLex_compat` (L3): replacing a sub-multiset `A` of `M` by a smaller `B`
  gives a smaller multiset; `degLex_clamp`: `clamp` never increases.
- `Rw_wf` (L4): one-step rewriting by an oriented rule set is well-founded.
- `Rw_sound`, `Rw_rtg_sound`, `rules_sound`, `Step_sound` (L5): a rewrite
  by rules that are `=_E`-consequences stays inside `=_E`.
- `merge_I2`, `Infer_I2` (L6): every inference preserves `I2`.
- `soundness`, re-exported as `completion_sound` (L7): every reachable
  state from an `I2` state is `I2`; no bound, no `I1`, `I4` or `I5`.
- `critical_pair_lemma` (L8): with `clamp` the identity, joinable critical
  pairs of overlapping rules give local confluence.
- `newman` (L9): well-founded and locally confluent implies confluent.
  Proved by well-founded induction; Mathlib has only the diamond form.
- `Rw_add`, `Joinable_add` (L10): rewriting and joinability are compatible
  with multiset union when `clamp` is the identity.
- `confluent_modulo'`, re-exported as `confluent_modulo`, and
  `critical_pair_lemma_modulo'` (L11, corrected): with the rules oriented,
  every law instance shrinking in `degLex`, `clamp` outputs normal for the
  law instances, and every critical pair of two distinct overlapping rules
  of the rules together with the law instances joinable under plain
  rewriting, clamp-normalized rewriting is confluent.
- `roundFixed_converged` (L12): a state whose round changes nothing, with
  `I0`, `I5` and `clamp`-canonical right-hand sides, is `Converged`.
- `completeness` (L13): under confluence, with the asserted equalities, the
  law instances, congruence and cancellation joinable, `=_E` coincides
  with joinability. `same_class`: in a converged confluent state whose
  right-hand sides are normal forms, `=_E`-equal active nodes share a
  class.
- `dickson`, `kapur_termination` (L14): Dickson's lemma for monomials over
  `Fin n` (`Pi.wellQuasiOrderedLE`), and Kapur's Theorem 2: a sequence of
  left sides that are irreducible when added and retired only by a
  smaller left side is finite.
- `Rw_mem`, `Infer_I4` (L15): every inference preserves the fixed summand
  pool.
- `run_stabilizes'`, re-exported as `run_stabilizes` (L16): along an
  infinite run of inferences the node set and the partition are eventually
  constant.
- `SlotState.reachable_I5`: from a state whose slots are least, every run
  of `mint`, `merge`, `shrinkRefresh` and `retire` keeps the slots least.
  `mint_I5`, `mintInto_I5`, `merge_I5`, `shrinkRefresh_I5`, `retire_I5`
  are the per-operation cases.
- `SlotState.shrinkNoRefresh_breaks_I5`: the repro over `Fin 7`
  (p < q < a < b, r; class {Add{a,b}, Add{p,q,r}} with slot Add{a,b};
  shrink Add{p,q,r} to Add{p,q}) satisfies `I5` before and violates it
  after, by `decide`. `shrinkRefresh_keeps_I5_repro`: the same shrink with
  the refresh keeps `I5`.
- `SlotState.state_I5_of_slot`: slot-level `I5` gives the completion
  state's `I5` through `Agrees`.
- `SlotState.fix_sufficient`: the `I5` hypothesis of `roundFixed_converged`
  is discharged by `reachable_I5`; the conclusion of `same_class` follows
  for any run of the fixed engine.
- `completion_complete_at_convergence` (`Main.lean`): `fix_sufficient` with
  confluence discharged by `confluent_modulo`. Remaining hypotheses are
  properties of the laws (law instances shrink, `clamp` is normal) and of
  the converged state (oriented rules, joinable critical pairs, `I0`,
  canonical normal-form right-hand sides, congruence-closed, `=_E` pairs
  joinable).

### Stub corrections

Two statements written from the sketch were false or weaker than needed,
and the proofs replaced them.

- L11 as sketched claimed local confluence from the rule-rule critical
  pairs plus the rule-law pairs alone. `stub_counterexample` in
  `Proofs/CriticalPairModulo.lean` refutes it by `decide` over `Fin 5`:
  rules `{a,x} → {p}` and `{b,x} → {p}`, a `clamp` that contracts `{a,p}`
  and `{b,p}` to `{p}`; from `{a,b,x,c}` the two steps give `{b,c,p}` and
  `{a,c,p}`, both normal and distinct, while the one critical pair is
  `({p},{p})` and no rule-law pair exists. The reason is that two clamped
  reducts give joinability to a common reduct on each side, and joinability
  is not transitive without confluence. The proved form follows Kapur:
  confluence of the rules together with the law instances as rewrite
  rules, by termination plus the plain critical pair lemma plus Newman,
  then transferred to clamp-normalized rewriting through normal forms.
  This needs the rules oriented and every law instance to shrink, which
  hold for identity drop, idempotent contraction, nilpotent clamp and
  inverse cancellation.
- L16 as sketched was planned through Kapur's termination theorem and the
  fixed pool. The proof needed neither: the abstract `Infer` relation
  already decreases the measure "fresh classes plus distinct
  representatives" or leaves nodes and `find` unchanged, so a run
  stabilizes by minimality of the measure. `kapur_termination` remains the
  statement about the concrete loop.

### Not formalized

- The engine's concrete completion loop (`cc_round`, `a_round`,
  `canon_repair_round`) is not modelled; `Infer` is the abstract inference
  relation of the sketch, and the measure argument of `run_stabilizes`
  bounds inferences, not the engine's rounds.
- The bridge from `State n` and `SlotState n` to the Rust data structures is
  the predicate `Agrees` and the per-operation correspondence described in
  the docstrings of `Slot.lean`; there is no extraction or refinement proof
  against the code.
- Retirement in `Slot.lean` assumes a strictly smaller active classmate
  exists when a node is retired (merge before flag). The engine's
  `cc_round` is believed to guarantee this; it is a hypothesis, not a
  verified fact, and the fix implementation must check it.
- Joinability of the `=_E` pairs at convergence (`hjoin`) and
  congruence-closure of the final state (`hcc`) are hypotheses of
  `completion_complete_at_convergence`; the sketch's induction that derives
  them from `RoundFixed` is not carried out.
- The A-only operators, inverse beyond pair cancellation, and completion
  interleaved with rewrite rules are outside the model, as in the sketch.
## Lean formalization status: e-matching

Project `lean/` (Lean 4, Mathlib v4.35.0), namespace `Semper.EMatch`.
`lake build` succeeds; `grep -rn sorry SemperProofs/` is empty for the
whole project; every final theorem depends only on `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

### Files

- `EMatch/Defs.lean`: the abstract e-graph (`Graph`: node list and
  `find`; `Node`: operator and children of one kind, tuple, sorted pair,
  sequence, multiset, set), the pattern type `Pat` (variable, plain, C, A
  with prefix and suffix rests, AC with element patterns and multiplicity
  annotations and a rest, ACI with a rest), the binding `Binding` (partial
  maps for classes, nodes, multiplicities, multiset rests, sequence rests,
  set rests), and the declarative relation `MatchC`/`MatchN`: the
  maximum-partition semantics, where an AC element takes one distinct
  child with its whole count and the rest is the complement.
- `EMatch/Ref.lean`: the reference interpreter over lists: the four index
  families as filters, `candidates` as their intersection, `decomposeAC`,
  `decomposeACI`, `expandA`, `decomposeComm`, the step type and `run`, and
  the left-to-right compiler `compile`.
- `EMatch/Lemmas.lean`: index exactness (four lemmas) and the multiplicity
  check (`mult_matches_sound`, `mult_matches_complete`), proved; pointer
  comments for the lemmas proved elsewhere.
- `EMatch/Proofs/JoinCommA.lean`: `join_complete'`, the commutative pair
  and the associative split.
- `EMatch/Proofs/DecomposeAC.lean`: the AC and ACI decompositions.
- `EMatch/Proofs/CompileWF.lean`: `compile_wf'`.
- `EMatch/Proofs/Interp.lean`: `interp_sound'` and `interp_complete'`,
  with the run invariant, the frame of a compiled plan, monotonicity of
  matching under binding extension, and the kind signature.
- `EMatch/Main.lean`: the final theorems under unprimed names.
- `EMatch/Check.lean`: the axiom audit.

The proofs total 4,962 lines (`Proofs/` and `Main.lean`).

### Final theorems

- `ematch_sound`: every binding the interpreter emits for the compiled
  plan of an application pattern satisfies `MatchN` at a stored node, the
  node bound to the root variable. Hypotheses in engine terms: node ids
  are distinct; every node's children have the shape of its operator's
  kind and the pattern uses the constructor of its head's kind (the engine
  keys `by_op` per operator with one fixed shape); the pattern's rest
  variables are distinct (a rest variable is bound once); the pattern's
  variables lie below the fresh counter.
- `ematch_complete`: every binding that satisfies `MatchN` at a stored
  node is emitted for the compiled plan, as a binding that agrees with it
  on every pattern variable; the emitted binding also carries the plan's
  fresh node and class variables. Hypotheses: distinct node ids, distinct
  rest variables, pattern variables below the fresh counter.
- `join_complete`: the candidate set of a join is exactly the identified
  nodes satisfying every lookup's predicate.
- `decompose_ac_sound`, `decompose_ac_complete`, `decompose_aci_sound`,
  `decompose_aci_complete`: the decomposition emits exactly the injections
  from element patterns to distinct children taking whole counts, with the
  rest bound to the complement; soundness needs the rest variable unbound
  on entry.
- `expand_a_sound`, `expand_a_complete`: the associative expansion emits
  exactly the splits into prefix, fixed middle and suffix; soundness needs
  the two rest variables unbound and distinct.
- `decompose_comm_sound`, `decompose_comm_complete`: both orders of a
  commutative pair.
- `compile_wf`: the compiled plan binds every variable before a step reads
  it.

### Stub corrections

Four statements of the first draft were false and are replaced:

- `expand_a_sound`: the reference interpreter writes a rest variable
  unconditionally. With the prefix rest already bound to `[5]` and no
  children, the emitted binding rebinds it to `[]` and does not extend the
  input; with one variable as both prefix and suffix rest and children
  `[1]`, the suffix write erases the prefix write. The theorem adds: both
  rests unbound on entry, and distinct.
- `decompose_ac_sound`, `decompose_aci_sound`: same cause. With no
  elements, rest variable `0` bound to the empty multiset and children
  `{1}`, the result binds `0` to `{1}`. The theorems add: the rest
  variable unbound on entry.
- `interp_sound`: a plain pattern with no arguments and a multiset node of
  the same operator: the join on `by_op` accepts the node, `MatchN`
  rejects it because the plain clause needs a tuple. The theorem adds a
  kind signature the graph and the pattern conform to, and distinct rest
  variables.
- `interp_complete`: the original conclusion asked the match witness to
  bind the plan's root node variable. With one node, a plain pattern with
  no arguments and an empty witness, the emitted binding has the node
  variable set and the witness does not. The conclusion is now agreement
  below the fresh counter on every map but `node`.

### Not modelled

- Scheduler plans: the theorems are about the reference compiler's
  left-to-right plan. The scheduler's reordering by selectivity, Phase A
  and Phase B, is not covered; the lemma list intended the plan as a
  hypothesis (`PlanWF`), and `compile_wf` proves that predicate for the
  reference plan, but the interpreter theorems carry their own invariant
  (fresh-counter monotonicity and freshness, which `PlanWF` lacks).
- Views under `:flatten`, the demand rule and the three bounds.
- Literals, sequence filter patterns, predicates, global bindings.
- The galloping cursor: indexes are sorted lists and the join is a filter;
  the cursor theorems are in the Verus development of containers-verus.
- The binary corollary, which needs `represents` from the
  congruence-closure development.
